# Weekly Planning Rhythm (Scheduling by Day of Week)

## Week start and phrasing

- The week **starts on Monday** (Monday is day 1, Sunday is day 7); when giving day-by-day advice, name the weekday explicitly (e.g. "Thursday: threshold intervals") instead of "day 1/2".
- A typical amateur training week has 3–5 sessions: 2–4 quality workouts (intervals / tempo / long ride) plus easy recovery spins or rest days.

## Placing quality workouts

- Keep **at least 48 hours** between similar high-intensity stimuli (e.g. VO2max sessions); put an easy day or rest day between two quality workouts.
- Put the long ride on the weekend or the day with the most available time; avoid heavy intervals the day before a long ride — heavy legs ruin long-ride quality.
- Back-to-back hard days: **2 days maximum**, with day 2 shorter and less intense than day 1, always followed by 1–2 easy days.
- Double-session days (long ride in the morning, intervals in the afternoon) suit only well-recovered advanced riders — not recommended in an amateur week.

## Reading TSB together with the weekday

- Monday is usually the weekly TSB low (weekend long ride plus accumulated fatigue); feeling flat on Tuesday/Wednesday is normal — not evidence of overtraining by itself.
- TSB recovers from Thursday onward, making Friday/Saturday good windows for quality work; especially true in the race week of weekend racers.
- When interpreting "Current Status (today's CTL/ATL/TSB)", check today's weekday first: attribute an early-week low TSB to the weekly structure before attributing it to overtraining.

## Recovery weeks

- Insert a recovery week every 3–4 weeks: total load (TSS) **down 30–50%**.
- Keep frequency (roughly the same number of sessions) while cutting per-session duration and intensity; trim the quality-workout sets first and keep the easy spins — complete rest actually slows recovery.

## Aligning with the current time

- The "Current Date & Time" section of the prompt gives today's date, weekday, time and timezone; base all "this week / next week / days to race" reasoning on it.
- Activity dates in the training library are UTC-based and may differ from the local calendar by one day; use local dates for future scheduling and mind the gap when citing past activities.
