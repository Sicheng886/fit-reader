# Heart Rate Zones and HR Interpretation

## Heart Rate Zones (5 levels, %HRmax)

| Zone | %HRmax | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Z1 | <68% | Recovery — easy enough to hold a full conversation |
| Z2 | 68–75% | Aerobic endurance; the main HR landing zone for power Z2 |
| Z3 | 75–85% | Tempo — breathing deepens but stays controlled |
| Z4 | 85–92% | Threshold — close to lactate threshold HR (LTHR) |
| Z5 | >92% | VO2max/sprint — sustainable only for short efforts |

## HR Drift (Aerobic Decoupling)

Definition: relative change of the "efficiency factor" (power/HR; speed/HR when no power data) between the first and second half of the session — it measures how stable the aerobic system is at constant output.

- **< 5%**: hallmark of a solid aerobic base; the cardiovascular system stays stable at that intensity.
- **5–10%**: mild drift — possibly insufficient warm-up, light fatigue, rising heat, or dehydration.
- **> 10%**: marked drift. Check causes in order: intensity itself too high (above the aerobic range) → fatigue accumulation (cross-check TSB) → dehydration/heat → carbohydrate depletion (late in long rides).

Drift only has standard interpretive value in steady Z2 riding; drift numbers from interval sessions or rolling terrain are weakly informative — check the nature of the ride before interpreting.

## Power–HR Cross-Interpretation

Power is the "output", HR is the "cost"; only together are they complete:

- HR 5+ bpm higher than historical levels at the same power: fatigue, early illness, heat, or caffeine/sleep issues — recommend deloading and observation.
- Time in power Z4+ significantly exceeding HR Z4+ share (systematic mismatch): FTP may be set too low (HR can't reach high zones yet judged high-intensity) or HRmax set too high; the reverse suggests the opposite. Re-verify athlete parameters against FTP estimation data.
- HR lags power changes by 30–60 seconds: in short intervals (<3 min) HR zone shares systematically underestimate actual intensity — judge interval sessions by power.

## Limits of HR Data & Complementarity

HR is affected by heat, caffeine, sleep, and emotion — daily swings of ±5–10 bpm are normal. With power data, use power as the primary anchor and HR as corroboration; without power (e.g. running without a power meter), HR + pace/speed efficiency becomes the main load basis — but state the correspondingly lower confidence explicitly.
