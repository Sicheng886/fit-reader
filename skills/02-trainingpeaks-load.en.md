# TrainingPeaks Load Model (CTL / ATL / TSB)

## Definitions

- **CTL (Chronic Training Load, "fitness")**: 42-day exponentially weighted average of daily TSS. Changes slowly; it is the measure of what training has built.
- **ATL (Acute Training Load, "fatigue")**: 7-day exponentially weighted average of daily TSS. Changes quickly; it represents recent fatigue.
- **TSB (Training Stress Balance, "form") = CTL − ATL**: positive = fresh (fitness > fatigue), negative = training under fatigue. Rest days participate with TSS = 0 — during time off CTL drifts down slowly while ATL drops fast.

## TSB Interpretation Scale

| TSB range | Meaning |
|---|---|
| > +25 | Overly fresh — possibly too long off training, fitness eroding |
| +5 ~ +15 | Ideal freshness for racing/testing (taper target) |
| -10 ~ +5 | Neutral — fine for normal training or low-intensity racing |
| -10 ~ -30 | Productive training zone: controlled fatigue accumulation, the main fitness-building range |
| < -30 | Deep fatigue: significantly higher injury/overtraining risk — schedule a deload |

## Safe CTL Ramp

Weekly CTL increase should stay under 5–7 points (roughly 5–10% relative). Sustained over-ramping is a leading cause of overtraining and injury. When evaluating load growth, look at the absolute increase first, then judge sustainability against the athlete's CTL base.

## Taper Principles

- Goal: race-day TSB in +5 ~ +15 (fresh without obvious fitness loss).
- Tapers usually last 7–14 days: cut volume (duration/frequency) 40–60%, **keep intensity** — retain a few short Z4–Z5 intervals to avoid detraining and "dead legs"; avoid complete rest.
- Final 48 hours: short low-intensity rides plus a few opener efforts (brief high-intensity wake-ups).
- CTL itself drifts down slightly during a taper (~0.5–1 point/day); that is a normal trade of a little fitness for a lot of freshness — do not panic.

## Intensity Distribution Types

Based on time shares of low (Z1–Z2) / mid (Z3–Z4) / high (Z5–Z7) zones:

- **polarized**: low ≥75% and high > mid. Lots of easy + a little very hard — the best-supported structure for endurance sports.
- **pyramidal**: low > mid > high. The most common natural distribution, suits most amateur riders.
- **sweet_spot**: mid-intensity share is relatively high. Time-efficient but costly to recover from; a long-term single-sweet-spot structure tends to fall into the "moderate-intensity trap" (not easy enough, not hard enough) — watch for fatigue accumulation.

Judge against the training phase: base building favors polarized/pyramidal; pre-race specialization may temporarily raise the mid/high share.
